Upfront Pricing

Straight Answers and Upfront Prices

Before we start any repair, we find out exactly what's wrong. Our technicians check the age and size of the tank, the burner or elements, the relief valve, the venting, and any signs of corrosion or seepage. Then you get an honest recommendation and the complete price up front, whether the job is replacing a thermocouple or replacing the whole unit. No hidden fees, no surprise add-ons.

Many water heater problems stay hidden until a failure is well underway. Sediment builds up on the bottom of the tank, the anode rod quietly dissolves, and the steel underneath starts to rust from the inside with no outward warning. If you notice rusty hot water, rumbling or popping sounds, a rotten-egg smell, hot water that runs out sooner than it used to, or moisture around the base, schedule an inspection right away. An early diagnosis usually keeps a repairable issue from turning into a flooded floor.

We also help homeowners plan ahead. Most tank water heaters last roughly 8 to 12 years, and a planned water heater replacement is always cheaper and less stressful than an emergency swap after the tank has already leaked. Our technicians know how hard water and local conditions wear on tanks over time, so we can tell you honestly whether your heater has years left or is living on borrowed time.

Preventive Care

Maintenance Plans for Morgantown Homes

Routine maintenance is the most reliable way to keep hot water flowing all year. Regular service heads off failures before they start, flushes out mineral sediment, and catches small faults while they're still inexpensive to fix. At Morgantown Water Heater, every maintenance visit is built around your actual equipment, covering a full tank flush, an anode rod inspection, a test of the temperature and pressure relief valve, a look at the burner and flue on gas models, element and thermostat testing on electric units, and a temperature check at the tap.

Many homes do best with one flush a year, and houses on hard water often need a visit every six months. The schedule flexes with the age of your heater, how hard your household works it, and local water quality, which controls how quickly sediment builds up and how long an anode rod lasts.

The savings add up faster than most people expect. A tank without a sediment crust heats water more efficiently, a replaced anode rod keeps corrosion off the tank walls, and a tested relief valve means the tank can safely vent pressure if something goes wrong. Next to the cost of replacing a tank early, a maintenance plan is a bargain.

Proper Sizing

Hot Water Sized to Your Family's Demand

Bigger isn't always better when it comes to water heaters. What matters for a tank is the first-hour rating, the number of gallons of hot water it can deliver in a busy hour, and for a tankless unit it's the GPM it can sustain when the incoming water is cold. We don't simply copy the size of the old unit. We look at how many people live in the home, how many bathrooms you have, and big draws like a soaking tub or a high-capacity washer.

Needs change over time. A growing family or an added bathroom can overwhelm the tank that used to be plenty. A tankless unit heats water on demand and never runs out as long as the flow stays within its GPM rating, while hybrid heat pump tanks use far less electricity than a standard electric tank. Whether you're comparing tank and tankless, or gas and electric, we'll explain the honest pros and cons in installed price, operating cost, venting and gas line needs, and installation work.

Frequent Failures

The Water Heater Repairs Local Homes Need Most

Most repair calls we get come down to a handful of familiar problems. The water never gets hot. The pilot lights and then goes out a minute later. The hot water runs out fast, the tank rumbles and pops, or a tankless unit starts flashing an error code. Rusty water, a rotten-egg smell, and a dripping relief valve round out the list. Our technicians handle all of them, starting with the exact part that failed.

The correct fix depends entirely on what actually failed. On electric tanks, that usually means an element or thermostat. On gas units, it's more often a thermocouple, flame sensor, or gas valve. Pressure spikes are solved with a thermal expansion tank and a properly set pressure-reducing valve, and we handle leak repairs at the drain valve and fittings before they spread. We also check the thermostat setting, since water above 120°F can scald kids and older family members in seconds. If the tank itself is leaking, no repair will hold, and we'll say so plainly. And if you ever smell gas near the heater, leave the house and call your gas utility right away.
