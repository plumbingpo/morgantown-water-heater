Most tankless problems announce themselves the same way, with a blinking code on the display and a shower that turns cold. Behind that code is a system with very little margin for error. There is no storage tank to fall back on, so the heater has to detect flow, light a burner or power its elements, and raise the water temperature by fifty degrees or more in the few seconds it takes water to cross the heat exchanger. When scale narrows the passages, when the flow sensor stops reading, when the flame rod cannot confirm a flame, or when the vent or gas line cannot keep up, the unit protects itself by shutting down. That is the pattern we see again and again in Morgantown, WV.

Catching a symptom early is often the difference between a quick part swap and a tank that gives out on the garage floor. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Moisture, rust, or drips appearing below the heater
  • Hot water that fluctuates even though nobody touched the faucet
  • Hot water that cannot keep up with more than one fixture
  • The display showing an error code instead of a temperature
  • Longer bursts of cold water between hot draws than you used to get
  • A combustion fan that sounds louder or rougher than normal
  • A heater that only fires when water runs at full blast
  • A clicking igniter with no flame, or a flame that lights and quickly drops out

What a Trained Technician Brings to the Job

Start with what is at stake. A gas-fired tankless heater can burn nearly 200,000 BTU per hour at full fire, and its vent is the only thing carrying carbon monoxide out of the house; a joint that pulls loose or an intake that gets blocked changes that in a hurry. Electric tankless models are serious electrical loads, frequently more than 100 amps spread over several 240-volt circuits. Then there is the diagnostic puzzle. Error codes point to a system, not a part, so a flow sensor fault, a scaled heat exchanger, and a bad thermistor can look nearly identical on the display, and guessing gets expensive.

A trained technician with the right instruments gets to the answer faster. Ours read the fault history, measure flame signal and igniter resistance, test flow sensor output, and check gas pressure under full load with a manometer, which is the only reliable way to catch a gas line that holds pressure at idle but sags when the burner ramps up. Descaling is done with a circulating pump and the proper solution, followed by a thorough rinse. Just as important, you get an honest opinion: if the heat exchanger is damaged on an older unit, you will hear it from us before you spend money on a repair.

Tips to Keep Your Water Heater Healthy

A few preventive steps can help you avoid a cold shower or a wet floor down the road. Put these technician-approved steps to work at home:

  • Get the supply line checked before adding gas appliances, which can starve a tankless unit
  • Leave the power on in cold weather, since freeze protection only runs when the unit has electricity
  • Protect the heat exchanger with a softener or scale inhibitor when your water is hard
  • Write down any error code before resetting the unit, and call if it keeps coming back
  • Flush scale out of the heat exchanger annually, and sooner in hard water
  • Make sure nothing blocks the exhaust vent or the air intake outside
  • Rinse the small filter screen on the cold inlet every few months
