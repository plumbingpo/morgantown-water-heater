Warning Signs to Watch For

Water heaters usually give plenty of warning before they fail, as long as you know what to look and listen for. These are the red flags our technicians see most often before a breakdown:

  • A planned switch from gas to electric, or to a heat pump model
  • A new jetted tub that outpaces the heater's capacity
  • Missing code items like an expansion tank, drain pan, or proper venting
  • An addition too far from the existing heater for quick hot water
  • Long waits for hot water at faucets far from the heater
  • A tank in a tight spot that is hard to service or replace
  • A remodel that moves the utility space to a new spot
  • Hot water running out before everyone has showered

Why Professional Water Heater Installation Matters

Installing a water heater from scratch involves gas, venting, electrical, and plumbing work all at once. Gas lines need sizing for the whole system's load, plus a leak test. Vents must meet the manufacturer's limits on length, diameter, slope, and clearances, because a bad vent can put carbon monoxide into living space. Electric, tankless, and heat pump heaters need circuits that match their load, sometimes more than an older panel can supply. On the plumbing side, the relief valve needs the right rating and a full-size discharge line, and depending on location, code may require an expansion tank, a drain pan, seismic straps, or a permit and inspection.

What you get from a pro is an installation that is done once. Our technicians plan the gas line, vent, and circuit before the first cut, take care of the permit when your jurisdiction requires one, and install the relief valve discharge and code items correctly. Every joint is tested, every gas install is checked for draft and carbon monoxide, and the temperature is set to protect against scalding. Rushed installs tend to leave undersized gas lines, bad vent pitch, and missing expansion tanks behind. Ours are built to pass inspection and to last. And if the simpler option is the right one for your home, we say so plainly.

Simple Ways to Extend Your Water Heater's Life

Routine maintenance is almost always cheaper and easier than an early replacement. Here's where to begin:

  • Plan an annual descaling if you installed a tankless unit
  • Have the anode rod inspected every few years to protect the tank
  • Glance at the pan and discharge pipe every month or two; water there means it is time to call
  • Store paint, gasoline, and solvents far from any gas water heater
  • Set the thermostat near 120 degrees for safety and efficiency
  • Start a flushing routine early, before sediment has a chance to harden
  • Leave working space around the heater, and never stack storage against it
