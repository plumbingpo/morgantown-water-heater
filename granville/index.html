Getting More Years From Your Water Heater in Granville

Routine maintenance makes all the difference between a water heater that lasts and one that floods the garage in year seven. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: a yearly flush, an anode rod check every three to five years, a quick relief valve test once a year, a thermostat set near 120 degrees, and annual descaling for tankless units on hard water, so problems are found while they're still inexpensive.

Equipment age matters just as much. With regular maintenance, a standard tank typically lasts 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts acting up, like discolored hot water, popping noises, or a damp spot around the base, it's time to plan a replacement. A planned replacement costs less and is far less stressful than an emergency swap after a leak.

If warning signs do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A dripping relief valve might point to excess pressure or thermal expansion with nowhere to go, both modest fixes. The same drip left alone for a season can wear out the valve and stress the tank until a seam gives way, and a small repair becomes a full replacement plus water damage. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and ruined floors.

Hot Water Headaches Granville Homeowners Face

Water heaters in and around Granville deal with a familiar set of stresses. Depending on whether a home is on municipal water or a private well, hard water may be part of the picture: calcium and magnesium drop out as sediment on the tank floor, insulating the water from the burner, which produces the rumbling and popping many homeowners hear when the heater fires. In tankless units, the same minerals build scale inside the heat exchanger, which is why annual descaling matters so much in hard-water areas.

Aging equipment is the other half of the story. A tank-style water heater usually runs 8 to 12 years, and by then the anode rod is often used up, leaving the tank exposed to corrosion. Homes here also run on a mix of fuel types, natural gas and propane units in some homes and electric in others, which means we service both sides: pilot assemblies, gas valves, and flues on gas units, and elements, thermostats, and breakers on electric ones.

Code requirements add another layer. Replacing an older heater often means bringing the install up to current code: an expansion tank if the water system is closed, a T&P discharge line that terminates correctly, venting that drafts properly on gas units, plus straps and a pan where required. Well water brings its own challenges, including extra sediment, iron staining, and the rotten-egg smell that sometimes shows up when the water reacts with a standard anode rod. All of these is fixable, and most cost far less when caught early. If something seems off, a diagnostic visit from our team catches the small problem before it becomes an expensive one.
